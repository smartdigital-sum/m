# Research notes — reference sites (4 Sept 2026)

What each site does on its homepage, in the order a visitor sees it. Use these as pattern references, not things to copy.

## School / college ERP vendors

**Fedena** — https://fedena.com
Nav: Product ▾, Partner, Pricing, Resources, About ▾, Contact, phone number, Free Trial button.
Hero: "College and School Management Software" → *Book a Live Demo* / *Pricing & Plans*. Numbers under hero: 40,000+ institutions · 200+ countries · 20+ languages.
Then: six module cards (Exam & Gradebook, Admission, Parents, Timetable & Attendance, Fees, Online Classes) + "50+ other modules"; why-us trio (easy / powerful / support); mobile app; integrations (biometric, GPS, payment gateway); testimonial carousel with institution names; 10-question FAQ; footer with company address.
Takeaway: the hero-promise + numbers + modules + testimonials + FAQ skeleton.

**Entab** — https://www.entab.in
Value-prop bullets; four case studies with quantified outcomes ("cut admin workload 70%", "tripled admission conversion", "45,000 students across 12 states"); full module suite; stakeholder diagram (school / student / parent / teacher / admin); security & compliance block; many "Book a demo" CTAs.
Takeaway: case studies must carry a number or a concrete "first".

**Skolaro** — https://www.skolaro.com
Hero: "Run your school on one platform — made smarter with AI" → *Book a demo* / *Explore the platform*; "Trusted by 1,800+ institutions across 4 countries" with a dashboard screenshot right under it. Numbers row; "five pillars" modules; six testimonials with designation + institution; lead form; footer with ISO 27001 and uptime badges. No pricing.
Takeaway: a real product screenshot under the hero is the trust signal; put the Jatiya Vidyalaya dashboard there.

**Edunext** — https://www.edunexttechnologies.com
Nav with a long Services submenu (ERP, Mobile App, E-Learning, Website Design, Digital Marketing, RFID…). Hero "1200+ schools"; lead form high on the page; tabbed offerings; company timeline; partner logos; "Why Edunext" tabs; 40+ client logos; awards; four named testimonials; sales CTA. No pricing.
Takeaway: a company timeline and a "why us" tab block work for an older company; for us the "how we work" steps do the same job.

## School-website agencies (closest to Smart Digital's size)

**SchoolPixel** (Patna) — https://schoolpixel.in
Nav: Services, Pricing, Portfolio, CBSE Compliance, Redesign, Maintenance, Cities, Blog, *Get Free Demo*.
Hero: "School Website Development Expertise in India — premium design, 25-module dashboard, CBSE-compliant disclosures, delivered in 7 days". Claims strip: free demo · 25+ modules · 7-day launch.
Then: live demo site; "Why SchoolPixel" (50+ schools, 18 states, compliance guarantee, free 1-year support); six benefits; "most school websites are stuck in 2010" pain block; features grid; dashboard preview; six service cards; three live demos; **public pricing ₹14,999 / ₹29,999 / ₹59,999 with add-ons ₹999–₹4,999**; comparison table vs freelancers / big agencies / DIY; CBSE checklist with free PDF; 7-day process timeline; blog; FAQ; lead form; footer with city and WhatsApp.
Takeaway: this is the closest model. A small regional company winning with transparent pricing, a process promise, a compliance angle and risk reversal ("pay only if you approve"). Our equivalents: ownership pledge, Assamese + English, on-site training, Sewa Setu-compatible.

**Inside Softwares** (Dehradun) — https://www.insidesoftwares.com/school-college-website
"Education website that inspires parents & increases admissions". Portfolio of five named schools with thumbnails and service tags; four service categories (websites, marketing, CRM, student suite); quote-request CTAs, no public prices.
Takeaway: portfolio cards = thumbnail + logo + tags + "view".

## General agency patterns

From Colorlib's 2026 agency roundup and similar: hero leads with work not words; case studies are structured (problem → build → result); services clearly categorised; trust = client roster + years + credentials; multiple soft CTAs rather than pop-ups; bold type, whitespace, one accent colour, asymmetric grids, restrained motion.
